Getting It Right

How Far Can a Blade Sign Stick Out?

Two things decide it, and both come from the city rather than from taste. The sign can only project so far over the public sidewalk, and it has to clear a set height above it so nobody walks into it. On a narrow sidewalk that limits how big the blade can be; on a wide one you have more room.

Not sure what your frontage allows? We measure it on the site visit and draw the sizes on a photo of your own building.

What Actually Goes Wrong With a Blade Sign

Blade signs rarely fail all at once. They fail in a pattern, and the pattern tells you whether it's a quick fix or a remake.

What you see
What it usually means
The sign tilts or swings
The bracket or wall anchors are loosening. Usually a re-anchor, not a new sign.
A section of the face goes dark
Water has gotten into an illuminated face. A service call, not a rebuild.
Colors look washed out
Years of sun have faded the face. It can be refaced without replacing the bracket.
One side won't light evenly
A driver or LED module has failed. A repair, not a remake.
Too high or too small to read
A sizing problem from the original install, not a fault. We re-spec and remount it.

Recognize one of these on your sign? That's usually a service call, not a replacement.

Adding One On

Adding a Blade Sign to an Existing Storefront

Most blade signs go onto a building that's already there, so the mount matters as much as the design. Brick, stucco, and a metal canopy each take a different anchor, and a heavier blade on an older wall sometimes needs a quick structural check before it goes up.

If you lease, the projection usually needs the landlord's sign-off too, since it hangs over the shared frontage. We sort all of that out before anything is built, so install day is just install day.

The Legal Part

Permits for Projecting Signs in LA

A blade sign hangs over the public right-of-way, so it carries approvals a flat wall sign never deals with. We prepare the drawings and handle every submission as part of the project. Here's what a projecting sign usually needs.

1

Sign Permit (LADBS)

Every projecting sign needs a sign permit through the LA Department of Building and Safety. We draw it up and submit it.

2

Encroachment Approval

Because the blade hangs over the public sidewalk, the city often requires an encroachment approval on top of the sign permit.

3

Electrical Permit

An illuminated blade is an electrical installation, so a lit sign adds an electrical permit and a UL-listed build.

Not sure what your zone allows? We check the address before anything gets drawn, and you can read more on sign permitting in Los Angeles.

Good to Know

Frequently Asked Questions About Blade Signs in Los Angeles

What's the difference between a blade sign and a projecting sign?

None, the terms are used interchangeably. Both describe a sign that mounts perpendicular to the wall and reaches out over the sidewalk. You'll also hear them called fin signs or hanging signs.

Do I need a permit for a blade sign in Los Angeles?

Yes. Because it projects over the public sidewalk, a blade sign needs a sign permit through LADBS and often an encroachment approval as well. An illuminated blade adds an electrical permit. We prepare and submit all of it as part of the job.

How far can a blade sign stick out, and how high does it have to be?

Both are set by Los Angeles code and depend on your sidewalk. The sign has to clear a minimum height above the walkway, usually around eight feet, and can only project so far over it. We size the blade to what your specific frontage allows.

Can a blade sign be illuminated?

Yes. Blade signs can be halo-lit or backlit so they read after dark. We build the lit ones as UL-listed electric signs with the wiring placed where it can be reached for service later.

Can you mount one on brick, stucco, or a canopy?

All three, each with the right anchor for the surface. A heavier blade on an older wall sometimes needs a quick structural check first, which we handle before install day.

Should it be double-sided?

On a two-way sidewalk, usually yes. A double-sided blade reads to people approaching from both directions, which is the whole reason to put a projecting sign up in the first place.
